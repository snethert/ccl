This is the first piece of real runtime engineering in the restart, and it is good. The C code in control.c is a faithful implementation of D5: even-to-odd CAS acquisition where a loser writes nothing, probe-then-load admission with roots retained, parity-only waits, final membership rescan after the first stop pass, owner re-admission after release, and reclamation gated on a later completed collection plus a terminal host outcome. The copying collector really moves objects, preserves the cycle and shared tail, updates C root records, emitted binding frames and the VSP result region, and poisons from-space so stale references trap rather than silently reading old data. I traced the wake and rearm ordering by hand and found no lost-wake window: outcome is durable and separate, the rearm clears only the low word, and the loop rechecks pending, outcome and wake before every wait.
Two design decisions that emerged from failures are worth keeping in the D5 record itself, not just in the fixture:
- The generation-guarded 64-bit wake pair, so a late final host wake cannot land on a reused descriptor. D5 currently says "the descriptor is not reused while the host can still write it" but does not say how that is enforced.
- The explicit active-request pointer, distinct from outstanding-request ownership, so an interrupt reaches a nested debugger wait. D5 does not have this concept yet.
Both belong in decisions.md as a protocol v1.1 amendment with a dated rationale, since D5 is supposed to be the authority and the fixture now knows more than the document.
Smaller observations on the harness:
- The two missing-notification controls are self-witnessing. The test injects the defect into its own host model, then proves the Worker is still blocked by counting waiters, then throws. That is sound, but the README's phrase "the ordinary progress oracle rejects" overstates it. Say what it is: a waiter-count witness.

- Only the kernel side is mutated for most controls. Host-side protocol mutants exist for interrupts but not for, say, a host that writes outcome after the final wake. Worth one more control.
